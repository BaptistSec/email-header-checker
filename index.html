Email Header Checker

Read reported SPF, DKIM and DMARC results. Check reply routes. Never treat a pass as proof that an email is safe.

Start with an unverified result. Pasted headers cannot prove who added them. The first Authentication-Results header is not automatically trusted, and a familiar authserv-id can be forged.

1. Paste headers

Use your mail provider's original-message view. Headers may contain personal information. Processing stays in this page: no uploads, storage, DNS lookups or signature checks.

2. Choose a reported result

Do this only if you know your provider's authserv-id and have confirmed which header it added. Ask your mail administrator if unsure. Selecting a matching name does not verify its origin.

What this tool cannot tell you

It does not verify DKIM signatures, query DNS, calculate DMARC alignment or decide whether links, attachments or a sender are safe. It ignores message-body content after the first blank line. Missing, conflicting or unsupported syntax needs manual review.

Mailbox parsing is intentionally limited to a single conventional ASCII address. Groups, multiple addresses, quoted local parts, international addresses and domain literals are left unresolved. ARC results and Received-SPF are not substituted for Authentication-Results.

SPF or DKIM can fail on legitimate mail. A reported DMARC pass may use the other aligned mechanism; this tool cannot verify that explanation. A malicious sender can pass checks for a domain they own.

Reference: RFC 8601, especially sections 1.6, 2.5 and 7.
